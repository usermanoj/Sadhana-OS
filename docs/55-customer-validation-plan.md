# 55 - Customer Validation Plan

## Objective

Determine whether the proposed proactive Sadhana experience solves a painful,
frequent problem for a reachable customer who is willing to change behavior
and pay.

This plan tests product desirability and comprehension. It cannot prove
long-term retention until a usable product is tested over time.

## Primary Hypotheses

### H1 - Problem

Busy, spiritually intentional professionals repeatedly lose consistency
because practices do not adapt to real schedules, energy, and disruption.

### H2 - Value

An adaptive daily plan with calendar protection is more valuable than a
configurable checklist.

### H3 - Recovery

A Minimum plan and nonjudgmental recovery loop increase return after disrupted
days.

### H4 - Effort

An assisted evening recap is materially easier than reconstructing and logging
the day manually.

### H5 - Trust

Users will share calendar availability and selected health context when the
benefit, scope, provenance, and controls are clear.

### H6 - Differentiation

The combination of grounded practice, adaptive planning, timely intervention,
and explainable learning is distinct from a habit tracker or meditation
library.

### H7 - Payment

A meaningful subset of qualified users will make a real paid-beta commitment,
not merely state that they might pay.

## Research Cohort

### Discovery Interviews

Recruit 15 to 20 people matching the beachhead:

- at least 8 with a regular calendar-heavy professional schedule;
- at least 8 already attempting meditation, yoga, journaling, or another
  intentional practice;
- at least 5 working parents or family caregivers;
- a mix of wearable and non-wearable users;
- a mix of explicitly spiritual and secular language preferences.

### Prototype Tests

Run 8 to 12 moderated usability sessions. At least six participants should not
have seen the current Sadhana OS application.

### Exclusions

Do not recruit only friends, existing enthusiasts, developers, or people who
want to encourage the founder. Record relationship and potential bias.

## Recruitment Screener

1. What practices are you currently trying to maintain?
2. How often does your schedule disrupt them?
3. What do you do after missing two or three days?
4. Which calendar and health tools do you use?
5. Have you paid for a wellness, meditation, productivity, or habit product?
6. Which description best fits you: professional, founder, working parent,
   caregiver, student, retired, or other?
7. Are you comfortable discussing how you plan personal practices? No journal
   content or health diagnosis is requested.

Qualified participants should experience disruption at least weekly and care
enough to have attempted a solution.

## Discovery Interview Guide

Keep discovery interviews focused on past behavior before showing concepts.

1. Tell me about the last time an important personal practice did not happen.
2. What else was happening that day?
3. When did you realize the practice would be missed?
4. What did you do next?
5. How did you decide what to carry forward or abandon?
6. Show me, if comfortable, how you currently plan or track the practice.
7. Which reminders do you ignore, and why?
8. When has a digital tool actually helped you act at the right moment?
9. What do you wish the tool knew? What should it never know?
10. How do you currently judge whether a practice is helping?
11. Which wellness or productivity products have you paid for or cancelled?
12. What made the paid product worth or not worth the price?

Do not ask "Would you use an AI spiritual coach?" before understanding the
existing problem. That question produces unreliable enthusiasm.

## Prototype Tasks

Test without explaining the interface.

### Task A - Understand Today

Ask the participant to explain:

- what the product believes about today;
- what it recommends;
- why;
- what is observed versus suggested;
- what they would do next.

### Task B - Adapt The Plan

Scenario: a meeting was added and energy is lower than expected.

Ask the participant to choose a Minimum plan, shorten one practice, and
reschedule another.

### Task C - Begin Practice

Ask the participant to start a three-minute grounding practice, pause it, and
finish.

### Task D - Recover

Scenario: the planned evening practice did not happen.

Ask the participant to decide what to do without repairing or preserving a
streak.

### Task E - Recap

Ask the participant to review observed events, correct one inaccurate
suggestion, mark one item unknown, and complete the evening recap.

### Task F - Understand An Insight

Ask the participant to explain a weekly pattern, its confidence, and the
proposed experiment.

### Task G - Control Privacy

Ask the participant to find why calendar access is requested, change from
selected-event context to availability-only mode, and disconnect it.

## Measures

Record:

- task completion without assistance;
- time to identify the next action;
- ability to explain recommendation provenance;
- confusion between observed, confirmed, suggested, and unknown;
- number of prompts interpreted as judgmental;
- recap completion time;
- privacy setting discoverability;
- spontaneous statements of value;
- spontaneous objections;
- preference between current tracker and proactive prototype;
- willingness to join a beta;
- willingness to make a refundable deposit or paid commitment.

## Decision Thresholds

These are internal go/no-go thresholds, not industry benchmarks.

Proceed to a focused implementation experiment when:

- at least 12 of 15 qualified discovery participants describe schedule
  disruption as a recurring material problem;
- at least 8 of 10 prototype participants correctly explain the next action
  and recommendation reason;
- at least 8 of 10 complete plan adaptation without moderator instruction;
- median assisted recap completion is 60 seconds or less;
- at least 7 of 10 distinguish observed from suggested data;
- at least 6 of 10 prefer the proactive concept to a tracker-first home;
- at least 5 of 10 accept availability-only calendar access after reviewing
  the controls;
- at least 4 of 10 make a credible paid-beta commitment at a tested price.

Revise and retest if comprehension passes but privacy, tone, or plan adaptation
does not.

Stop or reposition if the problem threshold fails or users consistently value
the existing tracker more.

## Pricing Validation

### Method

Use three progressively stronger signals:

1. Ask what products the participant currently pays for and why.
2. Present concrete monthly and annual offers after the prototype.
3. Invite the participant to a paid or refundable-deposit beta.

### Test Cells

- USD 8 monthly / USD 60 annual.
- USD 12 monthly / USD 80 annual.
- USD 15 monthly / USD 100 annual.

Do not test every price on the same participant. Record geography and purchasing
power before interpreting results.

### What Counts

Strong evidence:

- entering payment details;
- paying a refundable deposit;
- joining a paid pilot;
- referring another qualified buyer.

Weak evidence:

- "I would probably pay";
- newsletter signup;
- positive visual feedback;
- saying the idea is important.

## Concierge Pilot

Before deep automation, run a two-week concierge pilot with 8 to 12 qualified
participants.

The product or facilitator can manually:

- review the participant's stated availability;
- propose three daily practices;
- adjust the plan after disruption;
- send one evening recap;
- produce one weekly insight;
- invite one experiment.

The pilot should use no private journal content unless explicitly supplied for
that purpose.

Measure:

- plan acceptance;
- practices started;
- reschedules;
- recovery after missed days;
- recap completion;
- prompt dismissals;
- weekly usefulness rating;
- continued use after facilitator contact decreases.

## Four-Week Validation Schedule

### Week 1 - Discovery

- Recruit.
- Run 8 to 10 problem interviews.
- Synthesize recurring situations, language, and rejected solutions.
- Confirm or revise the beachhead.

### Week 2 - Prototype

- Produce high-fidelity mobile and desktop flows.
- Run five usability sessions.
- Revise Today, plan adaptation, recap, and privacy controls.

### Week 3 - Decision Testing

- Run remaining usability sessions.
- Test positioning statements and pricing cells.
- Recruit the concierge pilot.

### Week 4 - Pilot Start

- Begin the concierge loop.
- Review intervention burden daily.
- Record plan, recovery, and recap behavior.
- Decide whether Task 057 should implement Today 2.0 or return to positioning.

The concierge pilot itself continues beyond the fourth week.

## Research Ethics

- Obtain informed consent.
- Explain whether sessions are recorded.
- Allow participants to skip questions.
- Do not request clinical histories.
- Do not interpret distress or provide medical advice.
- Do not test coercive notifications.
- Compensate participants where practical.
- Separate contact details from research notes.
- Delete recordings according to the stated schedule.

## Privacy-Safe Product Analytics Plan

Future prototype or beta events may include:

- `daily_plan_viewed`;
- `plan_mode_changed`;
- `practice_started`;
- `practice_rescheduled`;
- `practice_shortened`;
- `intervention_dismissed`;
- `evening_recap_started`;
- `evening_recap_completed`;
- `observation_corrected`;
- `insight_feedback_recorded`;
- `calendar_permission_mode_selected`.

Events must not include:

- journal text;
- calendar titles;
- health values;
- practice names that reveal religion or medical status;
- voice transcripts;
- free-text intentions;
- AI conversation content.
